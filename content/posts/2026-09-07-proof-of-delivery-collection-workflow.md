Every carrier we've spoken with has the same ghost in their AR aging report: a load that delivered clean two weeks ago but hasn't been invoiced because nobody has the paperwork. Not because the driver lost it. Because it's sitting in a text message thread, or a photo roll, or an email that got buried under load confirmations from three other brokers. The freight moved. The cash didn't.

## Why PODs Get Stuck Between the Truck and the Office

The proof of delivery is the single document that turns a completed load into an invoice a customer will actually pay. Without it, most brokers and a fair number of shippers will hold the check, and they're not wrong to. A signed POD is proof the freight arrived, in what condition, and when — and it's usually the backstop against a shortage or damage claim showing up a month later.

The problem isn't that drivers don't get the signature. Drivers get the signature almost every time. The problem is what happens to that piece of paper or that photo between the dock and the billing desk.

Here's the typical path on a fleet still running dispatch off a whiteboard and a group text:

1. Driver signs, snaps a photo on the receiver's dock.
2. Photo sits in the driver's camera roll until end of shift, or end of week.
3. Driver texts it to dispatch, or forgets and hands it over at the next fuel stop.
4. Dispatch forwards it to whoever does billing — if dispatch remembers which load it belongs to.
5. Billing matches it to a rate confirmation, builds an invoice, and finally sends it out.

Every one of those handoffs is a place the document can stall. A driver's phone fills up and the photo doesn't send. Dispatch is buried in check calls and the forward doesn't happen. Billing gets a POD with no load number on it and has to play detective. Add it up across a fleet running 20 or 30 trucks a day, and you're carrying receivables that should have gone out a week earlier, for no reason other than the paperwork took the slow road.

## What a Same-Day POD Workflow Actually Looks Like

The fix isn't a policy memo telling drivers to text faster. It's removing the manual handoffs entirely, so the POD moves from the dock to the invoice without waiting on anyone to remember anything.

### Capture at the point of delivery, not after

The photo needs to happen at the dock, tied to the load it belongs to, before the truck rolls. That means the driver isn't hunting through a camera roll later trying to remember which BOL matches which stop — the capture is already attached to the right RateCon and the right customer.

### Route it automatically, not by memory

Once the photo is taken, it should land in the load record without a text message, a group chain, or a driver having to remember dispatch's number. This is the piece that actually collapses the timeline. A driver magic link — a one-tap, no-login link sent to the driver's phone for that specific load — lets the driver upload the signed POD straight into the load file the second the delivery is done. No app download, no password, no "did you get my text." It's in Techvia TMS the moment it's captured, sitting on the load record next to the RateCon and the BOL, ready for whoever does billing to see it without asking anyone where it went.

### Bill against a complete file, not a partial one

Billing shouldn't have to chase a POD to close out an invoice. When the document lands on the load automatically, the invoice can go out the same day the freight delivers — not three days later when someone finally forwards the photo. For brokers working with factoring companies or quick-pay programs, that same-day POD is often the difference between getting paid this week or next.

### Keep it organized for the claim you hope never comes

A POD that's filed against the correct load, with a timestamp, is also your first line of defense on a cargo claim. When a receiver disputes a shortage six weeks after delivery, you want to pull that document in thirty seconds, not spend an afternoon searching text threads across three dispatchers' phones.

## What to Measure Before You Change Anything

Before you overhaul how PODs move through your shop, look at your own numbers for thirty days. Pull your invoice dates against your delivery dates on the last fifty loads and calculate the average gap in days. Then count how many of those PODs came in by text message versus through a system. That gap — delivery to invoice — is the number that tells you whether this is costing you real cash flow or just an occasional annoyance. Most carriers running this exercise for the first time are surprised by how many loads sit three, four, five days past delivery before the invoice goes out, and how much of that delay traces back to one missing photo.

### A Note on Detention and Accessorials

The same capture-at-the-dock discipline that speeds up your POD collection also protects your detention charges. If the driver is logging arrival and departure times at the same moment they're capturing the signature, you've got the timestamp evidence to back up a detention line item on the invoice — instead of trying to reconstruct it from memory two weeks later when the customer disputes the charge.
